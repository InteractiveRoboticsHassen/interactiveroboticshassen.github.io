Cutting-edge · Technology Radar

Frontier robotics, Physical AI, and embodied intelligence.

A living overview of emerging robotics and artificial intelligence technologies across China, North America and Europe. The focus is not only on impressive robot demonstrations, but on the deeper technologies that make useful autonomous machines possible: robot foundation models, vision-language-action models, dexterous manipulation, whole-body control, reinforcement learning, digital twins, world models, autonomous vehicles, intelligent manufacturing, aerial robotics, surgical robotics, and more.

Updated September 2026 China + West Research + Industry Videos + Podcasts + Demos Open-source resources
The big transition: robotics is moving from manually programmed machines toward general-purpose Physical AI systems that perceive, reason, learn and act. The most important race is therefore not simply “who builds the best humanoid body?” but rather: who can combine scalable hardware, large robot datasets, foundation models, simulation, dexterous control, safety and real-world deployment?

Robotics & AI technology radar

Use the filters or search box to navigate the ecosystem. The list emphasizes technologies with the greatest potential to change how robots are designed, trained and deployed.

AI Global

Robot foundation models

Large generalist models trained across many tasks, environments and robot embodiments. The goal is to replace task-by-task robot programming with adaptable learned intelligence.

Leading directions include NVIDIA GR00T, Google Gemini Robotics, Physical Intelligence π, Skild Brain, Figure Helix, Unitree UnifoLM, AgiBot World models and LimX embodied models.

Gemini Robotics → Isaac GR00T →
VLA Global

Vision-Language-Action models

VLA models connect visual perception and natural-language instructions directly to physical robot actions. They are becoming one of the central architectures of embodied AI.

The frontier is moving toward longer-horizon reasoning, multi-step manipulation, whole-body control and cross-embodiment transfer.

◎ Global

Embodied reasoning & agentic robotics

Robots increasingly combine perception, memory, planning, tool use and action rather than simply executing a learned motor primitive.

Important examples include Gemini Robotics ER, LimX COSA and agentic industrial AI systems that coordinate high-level reasoning with low-level control.

H Global

General-purpose humanoid robots

Humanoids are being developed for factories, logistics, laboratories, retail and homes. Their human-like geometry allows them to operate in spaces originally designed for people.

The key technical challenge is no longer walking alone: useful humanoids require perception, manipulation, reasoning, safety, reliability and economical deployment.

↔ Global

Whole-body loco-manipulation

Walking and manipulation are increasingly controlled as one coupled problem. A humanoid may step, bend, balance, reach and manipulate simultaneously.

This is essential for real-world tasks in cluttered homes, warehouses and factories.

✋ Global

Dexterous hands & tactile intelligence

Multi-finger hands, tendon-driven mechanisms, tactile skins, force sensing and vision-based touch are moving manipulation beyond simple parallel grippers.

High-value tasks include cable routing, folding, assembly, tool use, delicate grasping and deformable-object manipulation.

RL Global

Reinforcement learning for robotics

Reinforcement learning allows robots to acquire locomotion and manipulation strategies through trial, evaluation and improvement.

Current systems increasingly combine RL with imitation learning, human demonstrations, world models and safety constraints.

▶ Global

Learning from human video

One of robotics' largest bottlenecks is physical interaction data. New systems learn useful priors from internet video, egocentric video, teleoperation and motion capture.

This is a major route toward scaling robot intelligence without collecting every behavior directly on a robot.

DB Global

Robot data engines

Companies are increasingly treating robot fleets as data-generation infrastructure.

Teleoperation, autonomous execution, failure recovery and human intervention all become training data for the next generation of policies.

∞ Global

Simulation & sim-to-real

High-fidelity simulators allow millions of robot experiences to be generated before hardware deployment.

Key platforms include NVIDIA Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab, MuJoCo, reinforcement-learning environments, digital twins and GPU-accelerated physics engines.

WM Global

World models for Physical AI

World models attempt to predict how physical scenes evolve when an agent acts.

They can support synthetic training data, planning, evaluation, imagination-based learning and safer testing of rare scenarios.

⚡ Global

Edge AI & onboard inference

Powerful low-power computers increasingly allow large perception and robot-control models to execute directly on the robot.

Local inference reduces latency and dependence on network connectivity while improving privacy and reliability.

⊙ Global

Multi-robot collaboration & swarms

Robots are evolving from isolated machines into coordinated fleets that share maps, tasks, policies and operational data.

Applications include warehouses, drones, inspection, manufacturing, delivery and coordinated humanoid teams.

◉ Global

Autonomous robot safety

As AI directly controls physical machines, safety must extend beyond collision avoidance to perception, planning, compute, fail-safe control and fleet supervision.

This is becoming a major frontier for industrial and humanoid deployment.

OS Open

Open-source robot learning

Affordable hardware and open models are making modern robot learning accessible to universities, startups and individual researchers.

Hugging Face LeRobot now combines robot interfaces, datasets, teleoperation, simulation, imitation learning, RL and VLA policies.

⇄ Global

Autonomous battery management

Continuous operation requires robots to autonomously charge, swap batteries or manage energy across long shifts.

This is particularly important for factories, warehouses, security robots and humanoid fleets.

Ecosystem comparison

China and the West: different strengths, same hard problem

🇨🇳 China

  • Extremely deep manufacturing and component supply chains.
  • Rapid mechanical iteration and cost reduction.
  • Strong actuator, motor, gearbox, battery, electronics and drone ecosystems.
  • Large number of humanoid startups experimenting simultaneously.
  • Significant robotaxi deployment through Baidu, Pony.ai, WeRide and others.
  • Strong commercial leadership in drones and expanding advanced-air-mobility programs.
  • Increasing emphasis on robot datasets, embodied models and open developer platforms.

🌎 North America & Europe

  • Strong frontier foundation-model research.
  • Leadership in AI training infrastructure, GPU simulation and large-scale model development.
  • Strong university-to-startup research pipelines.
  • Increasing enterprise humanoid deployments in logistics and manufacturing.
  • Mature ecosystems in surgical robotics, warehouse automation and autonomous vehicles.
  • Strong emphasis on safety engineering, validation and enterprise integration.
  • Fast-growing open-source robot-learning ecosystem.

These are broad tendencies rather than strict boundaries. Modern robotics supply chains and AI software stacks are highly interconnected across regions.

Research directions

Other technologies worth tracking closely

Soft robotics Compliant structures, artificial muscles, soft grippers and robots that safely adapt their shape to physical contact.
Continuum robots Snake-like and flexible manipulators for medical, inspection and confined-space applications.
Variable-stiffness mechanisms Robots that actively change compliance for safety, manipulation and energy efficiency.
Event-based vision Neuromorphic cameras providing extremely low-latency perception for high-speed motion and robotics.
Electronic skin Distributed tactile sensing for contact-rich manipulation and safer human-robot interaction.
Dexterous teleoperation VR, motion capture and bilateral interfaces used both for remote work and robot data collection.
Robot self-improvement Systems that detect failures, request intervention, collect corrective data and improve future policies.
Neural motion planning Learned planners that generate collision-free robot motion more flexibly than hand-designed pipelines.
AI-native digital twins Real factories and robots mirrored in simulation for training, optimization and predictive operation.
Field robotics Construction, mining, agriculture, energy infrastructure and hazardous-environment robotics.
Exoskeletons & wearable robotics Robotic assistance that augments human mobility, rehabilitation and industrial strength.
Home robotics The difficult transition from predictable factories into cluttered, constantly changing domestic environments.

Important perspective

A robotics demo is not the same as a useful autonomous robot

What should we actually evaluate?

Dancing, running, martial arts and highly edited manipulation videos demonstrate valuable advances in mechanical design and control—but they do not necessarily demonstrate economically useful autonomy.

The harder benchmarks are: robustness, task completion rate, recovery from mistakes, adaptation, manipulation precision, uptime, battery life, safety, maintenance cost, data efficiency and total cost per useful task.

This distinction matters in both China and the West. The long-term winners may not be the robots with the most spectacular demonstrations, but those that can perform useful work repeatedly with minimal human intervention.

Research watchlist

The questions likely to define the next phase of robotics

Can one model control many robots? Cross-embodiment generalization could dramatically reduce the amount of data needed for every new robot.
Can robots learn from internet video? Human video contains vastly more physical knowledge than all existing robot datasets combined.
Can robots learn continuously? Truly useful robots need to improve after deployment rather than remain frozen after training.
Can manipulation scale? Walking is visually impressive, but dexterous contact-rich manipulation is often the harder economic problem.
Can simulation replace expensive data? Better world models and physics simulation could radically reduce the cost of robot learning.
Can humanoids achieve useful uptime? Battery management, thermal limits, actuator reliability and maintenance will matter enormously at fleet scale.
Can Physical AI be certified safely? General learned policies challenge traditional deterministic robot-safety assumptions.
Will homes or factories come first? Factories are structured but require reliability; homes offer enormous value but extreme environmental diversity.
How fast will hardware costs fall? Cost reductions in actuators, hands, sensing and compute could be as important as improvements in AI.

Perspective

Physical AI may become one of the defining technologies of the next decade

Large language models gave machines the ability to manipulate symbols, text, images and software. Robotics adds a much harder requirement: intelligence must operate under physics, uncertainty, contact, limited energy and real-world consequences.

The frontier therefore lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence, mechanism design, control theory, computer vision, reinforcement learning, simulation, high-performance computing, sensing and manufacturing.

China and the West are approaching this frontier with different historical strengths, but the destination is increasingly similar: machines that can understand human intent, reason about a physical environment, acquire new skills and execute useful work safely and autonomously.

A useful way to think about the field: the robot body provides the physical capability; sensors provide perception; foundation models provide generalization and reasoning; control provides stable motion; and real-world data provides experience. The breakthrough happens when all five become one system.
