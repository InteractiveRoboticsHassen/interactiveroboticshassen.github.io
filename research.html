Research

Geometry, mechanism intelligence, and emerging computation for robotics.

My research investigates how robotic systems can be understood analytically, designed systematically, optimized intelligently, and ultimately made more capable through data-driven and emerging computational methods. The work spans mathematical mechanism theory, screw-theoretic kinematics, Lie groups and Lie algebra, parallel and reconfigurable robots, origami and compliant mechanisms, intelligent manipulation, physics-informed learning, reinforcement learning, industrial automation, and quantum computing for robotics.

Research philosophy

Structure first. Intelligence second.

A robot should not be treated only as a collection of motors and sensors. Its geometry determines its allowable motion. Its constraints determine its force transmission. Its architecture determines its singularities, workspace and stiffness. Its controller determines how these capabilities are exploited. And its data-driven intelligence determines how well it can adapt when analytical models are incomplete.

My research therefore progresses from mechanism geometry to analytical modeling , then to optimization and intelligent control , and finally toward physics-informed and quantum-enhanced methods .

Geometry → Kinematics → Constraints → Optimization → Intelligence

Central questions

What I study

  • What motions can a mechanism physically realize?
  • How do passive constraints affect actual mobility?
  • How can parasitic motion be identified and minimized?
  • How can parallel architectures be synthesized systematically?
  • How should geometry be optimized for workspace and dexterity?
  • How can learning improve systems without ignoring physics?
  • Can quantum algorithms help solve expensive robot optimization problems?

Methodology

Typical research workflow

01 · Architecture

Define links, joints, topology, actuation and task requirements.

02 · Geometry

03 · Optimization

Study workspace, conditioning, dexterity, parasitic motion and geometric parameters.

Add control, estimation, learning or quantum optimization where computational complexity requires it.

Research direction

Toward structurally intelligent robots

The long-term objective is not simply to build more complicated robots. It is to develop robotic systems whose geometry, mathematical model, mechanism architecture, learning system and computational strategy are designed together.

This means using mathematics to preserve physical meaning, learning to handle uncertainty, optimization to improve performance, and emerging computation to address problems that become difficult as robotic systems grow in dimension and complexity.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Mechanism Science} + \text{Geometric Robotics} + \text{Machine Intelligence} + \text{Quantum Computing} } \]
